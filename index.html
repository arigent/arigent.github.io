About me

I am a health economist and British Academy Postdoctoral Fellow at the Department of Economics & Public Policy, Imperial College London, where I lead an independent three-year research programme on drug repurposing. My research focuses on pharmaceutical innovation, health technology assessment (HTA) and access to medicines.

Before the fellowship, I was a Postdoctoral Research Associate at Imperial College London, where I was co-investigator on a Gatsby Foundation project on the productivity of UK biopharmaceutical R&D and contributed to the EU-funded Hi-Prix project on pharmaceutical innovation and equitable access. I completed my ESRC-funded PhD at the London School of Economics, studying the role of patient organisations in innovation and access to medicines for rare diseases.

My work uses both quantitative and qualitative methods, including econometrics, survival analysis, document analysis and interviews. It has been published in journals such as The Lancet Oncology, The BMJ, Social Science & Medicine and Value in Health, and has been covered by The Observer. Beyond academia, I have worked as a health economist at NICE, as a consultant for the World Bank, and in health economics and market access consultancies.

Research

My research asks how policy, institutions and stakeholders shape which medicines are developed, how they are valued, and who gets access to them.
